I. GDHP General Remarks on the Draft WHO Digital Public Infrastructure- The Global Digital Health Partnership (GDHP) is pleased to contribute to the consultation on the Draft Reference Architecture Guidance for Digital Public Infrastructure for Health (DPI-H), drawing on the insights of its members. The Guidance succeeds in providing a global reference framework for countries to design health data systems that are interoperable, secure, and scalable. Its modular structure and adaptable standards allow the architecture to be applied flexibly rather than as a rigid template, with countries taking up the components most relevant to their own circumstances. This flexibility also extends to pace of adoption: the guidance allows for incremental implementation based on a country's existing digital health maturity, a realistic feature given how widely that maturity varies across regions. The underlying design principles are a particular strength of the document. They are comprehensive and well-rounded, spanning governance, interoperability, sustainability, and related dimensions in a way that gives countries a solid conceptual reference point regardless of where they sit on the maturity spectrum. Furthermore, the inclusion of country and regional implementation examples adds considerable practical value. These examples help translate the architecture's principles into tangible experience, illustrating how the framework can play out in real settings rather than remaining purely conceptual. GDHP considers this guidance a comprehensive and valuable contribution that lays a strong conceptual foundation for DPI-H, offering countries a credible and adaptable reference point from which to advance interoperable, secure, and people-centred digital health systems in the years ahead. II. GDHP Suggestions for Strengthening the Draft WHO Digital Public Infrastructure- Health GDHP recognises the solid foundation established by the draft Reference Architecture Guidance for DPI-H, and its principal suggestion is to further strengthen the guidance's practical implementation dimension, including clearer prioritisation, maturity assessment, governance operating models and financing sustainability. This could also include more explicit consideration of how healthcare providers might be engaged in planning and implementing DPI-H, as well as additional operational toolkits to support countries in practice. Conclusion GDHP considers the draft Reference Architecture Guidance for DPI-H to be grounded in a strong and well-conceived basis. The comments set out above aim to further support its practical implementation guidance, technical clarity, and governance framing, so that it can serve as a clear and actionable reference for countries developing interoperable, secure, and sustainable digital public infrastructure for health. Contributing GDHP Members The GDHP contributions to the draft WHO Digital Public Infrastructure - Health reflect the collective input and expertise of participating GDHP members.
