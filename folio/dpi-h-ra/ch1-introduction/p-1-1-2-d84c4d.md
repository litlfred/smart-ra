As an evolving, publicly governed socio-technical infrastructure, these digital resources for health — encompassing shared systems, specifications, and enabling governance — leverage foundational DPI, are interoperable and reusable, and are built on open standards. They enable countries to autonomously manage the secure processing, collection, exchange, and use of health-related data to deliver essential health services and fulfil multiple health and public-health functions, creating public value across the broader health ecosystem — including people, communities, public and private providers, civil society, and subnational actors, rather than serving only central health-agency functions — while upholding individual data rights and ensuring equitable access. Consistent with the [Universal DPI Safeguards Framework](https://www.dpi-safeguards.org/framework), DPI-H is complementary to equitable assisted and non-digital access routes, ensuring that no one is excluded from care or public-health services where digital identity, literacy, connectivity, or devices are uneven.
