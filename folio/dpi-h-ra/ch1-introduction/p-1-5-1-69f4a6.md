The first goal is **building trusted data foundations**. The core principle is that health data should be "*captured once and used many times*”. Countries currently invest heavily in reporting systems where the same data is re-entered at multiple levels of the system, leading to high burden, low quality, and limited comparability. The architecture defines the shared infrastructure that enables a single, high-quality data capture to support clinical care, facility management, population analytics including CRVS and mortality, public health monitoring, and domestic AI development simultaneously. Trusted data foundations also preserve the mortality and morbidity statistical pathways required under the WHO Nomenclature Regulations, within their applicable scope and transition arrangements. They connect clinical documentation, medical certification of cause of death, and death registration to ICD-11 coding and reported statistics, with traceable responsibilities and derivations.
