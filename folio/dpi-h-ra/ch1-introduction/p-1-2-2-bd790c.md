A similar pattern emerges when examining foundational data exchange DPI. Health systems involve a wide range of actors — ministries, facilities, clinicians, programme managers, health coverage schemes, supply chain operators, researchers, and patients themselves — each with different informational needs that no single system can meet alone. General-purpose data exchange DPI are designed to move data securely between systems, and they are agnostic to the content they carry. This content-neutrality keeps the exchange layer simple, broadly reusable across sectors, and free of any single sector's business logic, while keeping accountability for that business logic with the institutions that own it. In the health sector, however, the exchange format must be combined with an agreed clinical information model and identified semantic content — for example, ICD-11 can supply both the terminology used to record and exchange a diagnosis and the classification used to report it, with the original clinical detail retained.
