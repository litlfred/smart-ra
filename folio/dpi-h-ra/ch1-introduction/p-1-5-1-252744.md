The second goal is **supporting a capable, well-distributed health workforce**. This goal combines the health-system perspective — ensuring that the right number and skill mix of trained health and care personnel with the right skills are in the right place at the right time to meet population health needs — with the health-worker perspective, in which every health and care worker is individually identifiable and verifiable, equitably deployed where needed, paid reliably and on time, and supported to maintain and extend their competencies across the course of their career while upholding labour rights (including accreditation, continuing professional development, fair remuneration, leave, and pension protection). At national and subnational levels, it gives health-system planners and policymakers the information needed to track active workforce capacity, monitor and correct imbalances across the health labour market, ensure equitable distribution across geographic areas, facility types, and skill mixes, and strengthen workforce and health-service planning, distribution, and retention. In alignment with the WHO National Health Workforce Accounts (NHWA) and the health labour market framework, the architecture distinguishes between personnel who are *licensed to practise*, *professionally active*, and *practising*, and covers both statutory licensed professionals regulated by health practitioner regulators and non-licensed or community health and care cadres (including community health workers, allied and auxiliary staff, multiskilled workers, provisional registrants, trainees, and volunteers).

Achieving this goal requires a coordinated system approach in which shared DPI-H reference infrastructure works alongside operational workforce, education, and financial systems as well as external statistical and administrative data sources of varying digital maturity and non-health primary purposes (such as population censuses and labour force surveys conducted by national statistical offices, civil-service registers, and national pension or social-security schemes). Within DPI-H, an authoritative **Health Workforce Registry** — governed in strict compliance with national data confidentiality, privacy, and data-protection laws and purpose-limited data minimisation — connects with foundational identification systems where available, health practitioner regulators, educational qualification records, the **Health Facility Registry** (using care-services discovery patterns that relate organisations, facilities, locations, healthcare services, and practitioner roles), and the **Terminology Service** (governing national occupation, role, specialty, credential, and scope-of-practice vocabularies mapped to international classifications such as ISCO-08 and ISCED). The shared registry separates verified educational qualifications and regulatory registration from current clinical competence, task-specific authorisation, facility privileging, fitness to practise, and scope of practice, and records only high-level activity, deployment, and regulatory licence status; detailed human-resource administrative records (such as extended leaves of absence, internal administrative or disciplinary actions, attendance, salary calculations, deductions, and pension administration) and detailed learning records remain in operational human-resource information systems (HRIS/HRHIS), payroll and public financial management systems, and learning-management systems (LMS).

Across these shared and operational capabilities, the goal is structured around five mutually reinforcing sub-goals:

- **Sub-goal 2.1 — Identity and verifiable credentials established:** establishing governed workforce identity, educational qualifications (classified using ISCED), and professional registration, licensing, or alternative programme/employer authorisation for every regulated and non-regulated health and care worker, linked with health practitioner regulators, foundational identification systems, and HR/payroll systems while separating registration from clinical privileging, current competence, and scope of practice.
- **Sub-goal 2.2 — Equitable deployment, distribution, and shortage mapping enabled:** providing high-quality workforce data for planning, deployment, retention, and shortage mapping by combining mCSD-aligned practitioner-role, organisation, facility, and service affiliations (including NHWA activity status and full-time equivalent capacity) with catchment population need, service demand, workload, facility infrastructure, and external labour-market data sources.
- **Sub-goal 2.3 — Reliable and timely remuneration:** linking verified workforce identity and active deployment references in the Health Workforce Registry with operational HRIS, contract, attendance, payment-authorisation, public financial management, and payroll systems over foundational payment DPI rails, while informing NHWA indicators on compensation expenditure, entry-level wages and salaries, and pay gaps.
- **Sub-goal 2.4 — Continuous competency and career development:** tracking qualifications, continuing professional development (CPD) compliance, specialization, and licence renewal across a worker's career by linking authoritative qualification and compliance references in the Health Workforce Registry with operational LMS, pre-service education, accreditation, and clinical supervision systems.
- **Sub-goal 2.5 — Safety, accountability, and privacy-preserving governance:** enabling patients, health facilities, payers, and health practitioner regulators to verify that a worker's registration, licence, or authorisation status is accurate and current — protecting against payroll fraud and uncredentialed care — while enforcing purpose-specific access controls, de-identified or aggregated planning views, worker transparency, and rights of correction and appeal.
