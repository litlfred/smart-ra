This appendix provides a fuller breakdown of the individual capabilities within each of the five governance capability categories introduced in 3.4.5. It is illustrative reference material rather than a specification: the categories (clusters) are the level at which the reference architecture operates, and the detailed specification of any individual capability is a matter for dedicated standards efforts and national programmes. Across all five categories, three elements recur and belong to none of them exclusively — the **roles** that carry out governance (stakeholders, owners, and authors, both technical and clinical), the **rules** that determine when and how changes may be made, and the **policy** that frames them.

**Stakeholder identification and analysis in Intake.** Within the **Intake** category, stakeholder identification must vary according to the **artefact type** (code system, national extension, language translation, value set, concept map, logical information model, profile, or implementation guide) and the **affected clinical, public-health, or administrative domain**. Intake triage should require a structured **stakeholder analysis** covering **authoritative agencies**, **clinicians** across affected specialties and cadres, **health programmes**, **implementers**, **analysts** and statistical reporting leads, **patients or affected communities**, **regulators**, **terminology specialists** (including **classification and clinical coding specialists**), and **data-protection or safety experts**, ensuring that all parties impacted by a proposed change are engaged before authoring and review pathways are assigned.

**Authoring and governance roles and separation of duties.** Within **Authoring and publication**, collaborative authoring requires distinguishing six core operational roles alongside institutional governance roles, with explicit **separation of duties according to artefact type**:

- **Content author**: drafts or updates candidate terminology concepts, value sets, mappings, data-model elements, or narrative guidance.
- **Domain authority** (clinical specialty society, public-health programme authority, national classification centre, or regulatory agency): defines authoritative domain semantics, clinical appropriateness, classification conventions, and business rules for the affected domain, working alongside the **programme owner** who is accountable for programme indicators and service-delivery alignment.
- **Technical modeller** (*model authority* / technical steward): designs and maintains logical information models, structural profiles, terminology bindings, and computable constraints in the LIMR.
- **Terminology specialist** (*terminology authority*, including **classification and clinical coding specialists**): curates code systems, national extensions, translations, value sets, and concept maps in the Terminology Service, ensuring ontological, lexical, and statistical-classification integrity.
- **Reviewer**: conducts independent domain/clinical, technical/implementability, and privacy/legal/ethical/equity evaluation of proposed artefacts against defined governance criteria.
- **Publisher** (*publication authority*): verifies that all required review, consultation, and approval gates are complete, assigns version and provenance metadata, packages distributable releases, and publishes artefacts to operational endpoints under the oversight of the **standards authority / governance body** and for downstream **implementers**.

Separation of duties requires that the **content author**, **technical modeller**, or **terminology specialist** who drafts an artefact cannot act as its sole independent **reviewer**, domain approver, or **publisher**; for each artefact type, authoring is separated from independent review, domain/regulatory approval is separated from semantic/technical conformance sign-off, and final **publication authority** is exercised only after all required approvals are recorded.

**RACI-style responsibility model across SGI capabilities.** To make decision rights explicit across the five capability categories, the following RACI-style model (**R** = Responsible for execution; **A** = Accountable decision-maker with approval authority; **C** = Consulted prior to decision; **I** = Informed of outcome) assigns institutional and operational authorities to SGI capabilities:

| **Collaborative authoring: structural artefacts** (logical information models, FHIR profiles, implementation guides) | **I** | **C** (terminology bindings & code-system constraints) | **R** (authors models, profiles & computable rules) | **C** (clinical data element & workflow semantics) | **C** (programme dataset & workflow alignment) | **I** | **C** (implementability & tooling feedback) |
