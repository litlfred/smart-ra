**How to read the mapping:** the spreadsheet and capability summary that follow trace each workforce sub-goal (`2.1`–`2.5`) through stakeholder value, business capability, and application service to the shared DPI-H components, foundational-DPI dependencies, and operational workforce-management systems required to realise the goal, noting where shared components are reused across other goals:

- **Shared DPI-H reference components versus operational workforce systems:** the mapping centres on three shared DPI-H components — the **Health Workforce Registry** (a core foundational registry reused across clinical care, surveillance, supply chain, and health financing), the **Health Facility Registry**, and the **Terminology Service** — resting on foundational-DPI identity, trust/verifiable credentials, payment rails, data exchange, and single sign-on services. However, these shared registries alone cannot deliver Goal 2 without the operational systems that consume and update them: Human Resources for Health Information Systems (HRHIS) and employer HRIS, civil-service and facility payroll, contract management, public financial management (PFM), time and attendance, shift scheduling, deployment management, professional regulatory council platforms, clinical privileging and supervision tools, pre-service education and accreditation systems, learning-management systems (LMS), and workforce-planning and health labour market modelling applications. In particular, foundational payment DPI provides disbursement rails but does not determine employment validity, salary calculation, attendance, deductions, payment authorisation, or budget availability (which belong to HR, contract, attendance, payroll, and PFM systems); similarly, the Health Workforce Registry holds authoritative qualification and high-level continuing professional development (CPD) compliance references, whereas detailed learning activities, curricula, supervision logs, and competency assessments belong to LMS, education, and accreditation systems.
- **Care-services discovery model and dual national/international terminology governance:** linking workforce and service delivery requires more than joining a worker identifier to a facility identifier. The mapping aligns the Health Workforce Registry, Health Facility Registry, and Terminology Service with a full care-services discovery model — such as the IHE Mobile Care Services Discovery (mCSD) profile — capturing the distinct relationships among *Organisation* (employer, regulator, or provider organisation), *Practitioner* (regulated or non-regulated worker), *PractitionerRole* (role, specialty, authorised scope of practice, task-sharing authority, contract/FTE basis, and active period), *Location / Facility*, and *HealthcareService*. Because international classifications alone cannot represent country-specific cadres, multiskilled workers, community health work, task sharing, or local scope of practice, the Terminology Service governs national occupation, role, specialty, credential, and scope-of-practice vocabularies separately and maintains structured mappings to international classifications — **ISCO-08** (WHO health worker classification framework) for occupations and **ISCED** (International Standard Classification of Education) for educational qualifications — alongside interoperability with multi-source national data (regulatory registers, censuses, labour force surveys, and pension schemes) to populate NHWA indicators across *licensed to practise*, *professionally active*, and *practising* statuses.
- **Boundary of registry data, privacy compliance, and purpose-limited reuse:** the Health Workforce Registry must comply with national data confidentiality, privacy, and data-protection laws. Detailed HR administrative records (such as extended leaves of absence, internal administrative or disciplinary proceedings, performance appraisals, and pay/deduction schedules) are held in employer HRIS and payroll systems; the shared registry records only the resulting high-level operational deployment status and regulator-issued licence/authorisation status. To prevent unnecessary exposure of personal residential addresses, nationality, disciplinary histories, salary details, or employment terms during payroll validation or workforce planning, the architecture enforces purpose-specific datasets and role-based access controls, provides de-identified or aggregated datasets for workforce planning and NHWA reporting, and gives health workers audit transparency into access to their records together with formal mechanisms for correction and appeal.
- **Suggested phased implementation sequence:** countries can sequence these capabilities progressively as infrastructure matures: (1) *Phase 1 — Governed workforce identity, classification, and registration/authorisation status*, establishing unique workforce identifiers across licensed and non-licensed/CHW cadres, governed national role and qualification terminology mapped to ISCO-08 and ISCED, authoritative registration/authorisation status, and privacy, correction, and appeal controls; (2) *Phase 2 — Organisational affiliations and mCSD care-services discovery*, linking Practitioner, PractitionerRole, Organisation, Facility/Location, and HealthcareService records with NHWA activity statuses (*licensed to practise*, *professionally active*, *practising*) and contract/FTE data; (3) *Phase 3 — Workforce planning analytics, LMS/CPD integration, and payroll/HR validation*, combining aggregated workforce, facility, service-demand, workload, catchment-population, and external statistical/pension data for shortage mapping, connecting LMS/accreditation systems for CPD compliance, and integrating HRIS, attendance, PFM, and payroll systems for payee validation and NHWA compensation reporting; and (4) *Phase 4 — Cross-border and regional credential verification and mobility reporting*, enabling bilateral or regional verification of credentials and ethical recruitment monitoring.
