| **GOVERNANCE** | Governance of the Product Registry follows the cross-cutting governance framework set out in Section 3.4 of the guidance document. Component-specific considerations include:<br>(1) **Regulatory authority alignment, service levels, and urgent propagation:** The governance framework must explicitly state whether the Product Registry acts directly as the regulatory source, as a national master-data hub, or as a federated reference view linking authoritative registers. Where the national medicines or medical-device regulatory authority maintains a separate market-authorisation register, formal governance and operational agreements must define source authority, data-sharing and event-notification methods, maximum update latency (service levels), delivery acknowledgement, scheduled reconciliation, conflict-escalation and incident-management procedures, and immediate priority propagation pathways for urgent regulatory actions including recalls, batch quarantines, licence suspensions, and market withdrawals.<br>(2) **Country source strategy and attribute-level precedence:** External data sources — illustrated by national regulatory registers, WHO prequalification listings, national procurement and donor catalogues, medical-device registries, international product databases, manufacturer submissions, GDSN data pools, WHODrug, and ISO IDMP implementations — should be onboarded conditionally under a documented country source strategy that assesses source authority, licensing, availability, verification, and fallback options. Rather than selecting a single source for an entire product record, the governance framework must define precedence at the **attribute level** (for example, designating the national regulatory authority as authoritative for market authorisation, regulatory status, and approved clinical identity attributes, while allowing verified manufacturer or GS1/GDSN sources to govern selected trade-item, GTIN, packaging hierarchy, and volumetric attributes), preserve attribute-level provenance, and retain and flag unresolved cross-source conflicts for human stewardship resolution.<br>(3) **Submission trust, role separation, and attribute-specific approval workflows:** Governance workflows must enforce organisational and credential verification for external submitters, attribute-based permissions, submission provenance, digital signatures where appropriate, and strict separation of submitter and approver roles, using attribute- and product-class-specific approval routing across regulatory, clinical, logistics, and master-data stewards.<br>(4) **Downstream lifecycle coordination:** Lifecycle management for product records (activation, deprecation, suspension, withdrawal, and retirement) must be coordinated with the Product Catalogue Management Tool steward, since status changes in a registry record affect all downstream catalogue entries that reference it. |
