Health systems generate substantial volumes of data through the daily delivery of care — patient encounters, diagnoses, treatments, laboratory results, immunisations, referrals, and community health interactions. In the majority of health systems, this data does not flow naturally into the information that health system managers and policymakers need to make decisions. Health workers are instead required to manually compile and submit reports, extracting from registers and patient records information that was already captured at the point of care, reformatting it for each programme that requests it, and submitting it through parallel channels that operate independently. The result is a system in which the people who deliver care spend significant time producing information for those who manage it, while the information produced is frequently incomplete, delayed, and difficult to compare across facilities, programmes, or time.
