- The Client Registry provides the shared person-identity resolution layer and does not store scheme membership, contribution status, exemption category, or benefit entitlement; likewise, the Benefits Package Registry holds population-level benefit and scheme definitions rather than person-level enrolment records, while operational Beneficiary Registries and enrolment systems record which individuals are enrolled in which schemes, reconciling those records against the canonical Client Registry identifier and linking them to the relevant package version in the Benefits Package Registry.
- The Health Facility Registry and Health Workforce Registry provide the authoritative records of facility and practitioner identity, licensing, service capabilities, and professional qualification status. The Benefits Package Registry references general facility types, care levels, and practitioner qualification categories to specify where and by whom covered services may be delivered, whereas provider-specific empanelment, payer-provider contracting, service authorisation, and payment eligibility are operational financing relationships maintained in Contract and Benefits Management Systems that reference authoritative facility and workforce identifiers.
- The Product Registry provides the standardised catalogue of medicines and health commodities; benefit package definitions that include medicine or commodity coverage reference authoritative product identifiers from that registry rather than maintaining parallel lists, while operational financing systems combine those identifiers with negotiated tariffs, fee schedules, and reimbursement rules.
- The Terminology Service provides the standardised coding of services, procedures, and diagnoses that benefit definitions use to specify covered items unambiguously — an essential foundation that operational claims and reimbursement systems combine with clinical coding rules, bundling logic, and fee schedules to validate and price claims consistently.
