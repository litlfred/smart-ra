The Product Registry is the national authoritative reference for core identity, classification, and regulatory-recognition data for health products — medicines, vaccines, medical devices, diagnostics, and health commodities — recognised for use within the national health system. Depending on national legal mandates and institutional architecture, a country's Product Registry may operate in one of three models: (a) directly as the regulatory source maintained by the national medicines or medical-device regulatory authority; (b) as a national product master-data hub that consolidates, reconciles, and publishes records drawn from one or more authoritative registers; or (c) as a federated reference view that links distinct authoritative sources (for example, separate medicines and medical-device market-authorisation registers, national procurement catalogues, and global identifier directories) behind a unified query and identifier-resolution interface. Where a medicines or medical-device regulator maintains a separate legally authoritative market-authorisation register, that regulatory register remains the legal source of truth for market authorisation and regulatory status, while the Product Registry governs cross-sectoral master-data publication and interoperability under explicit attribute-level authority, provenance, and precedence rules. Across all three models, it maintains a standardised, uniquely identified, and governed record for each product, providing the common reference point against which supply chain, clinical, customs, reimbursement, and regulatory systems can consistently identify and classify what they are ordering, storing, dispensing, or reporting on.
