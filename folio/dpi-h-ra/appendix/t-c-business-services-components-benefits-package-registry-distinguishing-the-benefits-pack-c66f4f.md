| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Architecture layer**<br>Where it sits in the DPI-H framework | Business Services Layer — reusable, shared reference publication component with DPI-H characteristics. Directly adjacent to the core registry layer; qualifies as DPI-H shared reference infrastructure. | Financial Domain Functional Application — operational system managing scheme-specific contracting, eligibility, pre-authorisation, claims adjudication, and payment workflows. Outside DPI-H; country- and scheme-specific implementation. | Financial Domain Functional Application — operational scheme-management component holding person-specific enrolment and membership data. Outside DPI-H; managed by scheme operators and health financing institutions. |
| **Core question answered**<br>The fundamental problem each component exists to solve | What benefit packages exist — what do they cover, who defines them, who finances them, and under what general population-level conditions are they available?<br>Example: "What services does the Maternal Health Voucher Programme cover, who funds it, and which facility tiers and practitioner categories may provide them?" | How are benefit packages administered operationally — who has contracted with whom, which specific providers are empanelled, what are the negotiated tariffs and payment terms, and how are pre-authorisations, claims, contributions, and payment instructions processed?<br>Example: "Is Facility X contractually empanelled for the Maternal Health Voucher Programme, at what reimbursement tariff, and does this submitted claim satisfy clinical and evidentiary requirements for payment?" | Who is enrolled — which specific individuals and households are covered by which schemes, from when to when, under what contribution or exemption terms, and what is their current utilisation status?<br>Example: "Is this patient currently enrolled in the Maternal Health Voucher Programme, and how much of her annual utilisation cap remains?" |
| **What it manages**<br>The artefacts it stores, governs, and publishes | Authoritative benefit package definitions and testable machine-readable reference rules: covered services, procedures, medicines, devices, exclusions, co-payment parameters, utilisation caps, annual limits, general eligibility criteria, payer attribution, plain-language summaries, and facility-tier linkages. | Payer-provider contracts and provider empanelment records (referencing the Health Facility and Health Workforce Registries); negotiated tariffs, fee schedules, and payment methods; premium and contribution schedules; executable pre-authorisation and claims adjudication rules; claims submissions, evidentiary assessments, adjudication outcomes, dispute records, and payment instructions. | Individual and household enrolment records: which person is enrolled in which scheme (reconciled against the canonical Client Registry identity), enrolment start and end dates, premium, contribution, or social-protection exemption status, household or family group linkage, benefit utilisation balances, and scheme-specific beneficiary identifiers. |
| **Relationship to the Client Registry**<br>How each component uses the shared identity layer | Does not reference individual person records in the Client Registry. Publishes scheme and benefit definitions at the population category level — which schemes exist and what general criteria define eligibility — without recording individual enrolment. | Consumes the Client Registry to verify canonical person identity during enrolment, eligibility, and claims transactions. Any country-specific CBMS implementation must reconcile enrolment and claims records against the shared Client Registry identity and support a standard cross-scheme check for duplicate enrolment to provide a common baseline for eligibility verification and integrity controls across schemes. | Directly links scheme-specific enrolment records to the canonical Client Registry identifier. Holds person-level scheme membership, contribution, and exemption attributes that must not be stored in the Client Registry itself, while reconciling against the Client Registry to support cross-scheme duplicate-enrolment checks. |
| **When it is used**<br>Design-time reference vs runtime operational | Design-time authoring and governance, plus runtime reference publication. Queried or synchronised by operational systems that need current or historical package definitions and reference rule artefacts.<br>Example: A claims or eligibility system references the published package version in the registry to check whether a service code is on the covered list and what co-payment parameter applies. | Operational runtime. Active throughout every transaction cycle — enrolment, provider empanelment, contracting, pre-authorisation, claims submission, evidentiary review, adjudication, payment instruction, audit, and dispute resolution.<br>Example: A provider submits a claim; the CBMS verifies provider empanelment and contract tariffs, evaluates the claim and supporting service-delivery evidence against adjudication rules, and issues an authorised payment instruction to payment rails. | Operational runtime. Queried and updated during enrolment, point-of-care eligibility verification, and claims validation to confirm that a specific individual is actively enrolled in the relevant scheme and within their benefit limits.<br>Example: A provider queries the Beneficiary Registry (via the CBMS or an eligibility service) at point of care to confirm that the patient is actively enrolled before rendering a covered service. |
| **What it does NOT do**<br>The boundaries of each component's scope | Does not enrol individuals or store person-level membership. Does not execute person-specific eligibility determinations. Does not manage provider empanelment, payer-provider contracts, or negotiated fee schedules. Does not execute prior authorisation, adjudicate claims, assess service-delivery evidence, or initiate payments. | Does not author the canonical national definition of what benefit packages exist (that is the BPR's role). Does not hold canonical person, facility, workforce, or product identity records (those sit in the core DPI-H registries). Does not replace foundational payment settlement rails or public financial management systems. | Does not define what benefit packages exist (that is the BPR's role). Does not manage provider contracts, tariffs, or claims adjudication (that is the CBMS's role). Does not replace the canonical health identity record in the Client Registry. |
| **Relationship to each other**<br>How the three components depend on and interact with one another | The BPR is the authoritative reference source consumed by both the CBMS and the Beneficiary Registry. When the CBMS configures a payer-provider contract or validates a claim, it references the package definition version in the BPR. When the Beneficiary Registry records an enrolment, it links the individual's enrolment record to a specific package version in the BPR. | The CBMS consumes the BPR (for authoritative coverage definitions and reference rules) and the Beneficiary Registry (to confirm person-specific enrolment and utilisation balances), alongside the Client, Facility, Workforce, and Product Registries. Enrolment and claims transactions processed by the CBMS update the individual's record in the Beneficiary Registry and are reconciled against the Client Registry identity. | The Beneficiary Registry references the BPR to associate each enrolled person with the package version governing their entitlement, reconciles person identity with the Client Registry, receives enrolment and utilisation updates from the CBMS, and supplies person-specific membership status to point-of-care and claims workflows. |
| **FHIR alignment**<br>Relevant FHIR resources for each component (FHIR R6) | `InsuranceProduct` — the general definition of a benefit product. `InsurancePlan` — a product configured for a specific scheme or population. These are design-time, reference-level resources (paired with computable rule artefacts where appropriate). | `Coverage` (in operational transaction context), `Claim`, `ClaimResponse`, `CoverageEligibilityRequest`, `CoverageEligibilityResponse`, `Contract` (for payer-provider agreements), and `ExplanationOfBenefit`. | `Coverage` (person-specific instance linking an identified individual to a specific plan and validity period), `Group` (for household or family coverage structures), and `EpisodeOfCare` (where scheme coverage is tied to care episodes). |
| **Risk if absent**<br>The specific failure that results if each component is not in place | No shared definition of what is covered: every claims system, eligibility engine, and provider portal maintains its own local copy of benefit definitions, which diverge over time. Coverage disputes multiply; cross-scheme analysis becomes impossible; coverage gaps are invisible to governments. | Benefits cannot be administered operationally: without a system to manage provider contracts and tariffs, verify eligibility, assess service-delivery evidence, adjudicate claims, and authorise payments, the benefit package remains a policy document with no operational mechanism for purchasing or reimbursement. | No individual-level enrolment record: providers cannot confirm at point of care whether a specific person is enrolled; claims cannot be verified against active membership; and cross-scheme reconciliation to detect duplicate enrolment becomes difficult to perform. |
| **Primary users**<br>The roles and institutions that interact directly with each component | Ministry of Health (package steward); health financing analysts and planners; scheme regulators; operational eligibility and claims systems (consuming reference definitions via API); beneficiary-facing portals and assisted-access channels. | Health insurance agencies and SHP bodies; private insurers; healthcare providers submitting pre-authorisations and claims; finance departments authorising payments; clinical auditors, anomaly-detection, investigation, appeal, and oversight bodies. | Health insurance agencies and SHP bodies (managing enrolment); social-protection linkages updating eligibility status; beneficiaries verifying their own membership; providers and claims systems querying active enrolment. |
