Within **Authoring and publication**, effective semantic governance requires that review, consultation, and approval capabilities be differentiated by expertise, impact, and institutional mandate rather than treated as a single generic sign-off:

- **Clinical and domain review.** Clinical review cannot be treated as one uniform step because required expertise differs by medical or health specialty, profession, target population, care setting, and use case. Governance rules should define **domain-specific reviewer qualifications** (spanning relevant medical specialties, nursing, midwifery, pharmacy, laboratory medicine, public health, community health, and **classification and clinical coding specialists**), mandatory **conflict-of-interest disclosure and recusal rules**, **multidisciplinary review panels** where artefacts span multiple professions or programmes, and explicit **quorum and decision rules** before clinical sign-off is granted.
- **Technical and implementability review.** Technical review must extend beyond syntactic or schema correctness to evaluate end-to-end **implementability and validation**, covering **formal structural and profile validation**, **cross-artefact dependency and version-pinning checks**, **terminology binding and value-set expansion resolution**, **runtime performance and scalability impact**, **security and access-control considerations**, **backwards compatibility and data-migration pathways**, **validated implementation examples**, and **conformance-test readiness** (including executable test fixtures and assertions).
- **Policy, legal, privacy, human-rights, ethical, and equity review.** Because semantic artefacts may encode sensitive clinical classifications, stigmatising diagnoses, protected demographic or social attributes, benefit-eligibility or reimbursement restrictions, or mandatory surveillance concepts, artefacts with material individual or population impact require dedicated **privacy, human-rights, legal, ethical, and health-equity review** prior to approval to prevent discriminatory exclusion, unlawful data processing, or disproportionate surveillance harm.
- **Proportionate public consultation.** Rather than leaving external consultation absent or purely optional, SGI governance should require **proportionate public consultation** for high-impact national artefacts (such as core national data models, mandated code systems and extensions, cross-programme value sets, and national implementation guides), providing **accessible consultation materials** (including plain-language summaries alongside technical artefacts), defined review windows, **publication of submitted responses**, and a transparent **explanation of disposition** for how comments were accepted, modified, or declined.
- **Approval authority and legal mandate.** **Approval authority** must be explicitly tied to **artefact ownership and legal or institutional mandate**, defining who approves each artefact type (national extensions, translations, value sets, concept maps, logical models, and implementation guides) and separating **clinical/programme approval**, **semantic and classification approval**, **technical and model approval**, **regulatory or statutory approval** (where regulation, reimbursement, or mandatory reporting applies), and final **publication approval** where necessary, supported by documented conflict-resolution, escalation, appeals, and emergency change procedures.
