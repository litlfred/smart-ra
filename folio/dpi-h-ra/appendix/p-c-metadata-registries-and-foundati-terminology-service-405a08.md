The incentives that produce this problem are strong. Individual systems have limited visibility of or interest in how their local code choices affect other systems' ability to interpret the same data. Without a shared infrastructure that makes the semantic consistent coding available as a service, the path of least resistance is to define codes locally and map to standards only when forced to by specific exchange requirements. A national Terminology Service makes the consistent choice the easy choice: consuming systems query the TS rather than maintaining their own code lists, and the meaning of coded values is determined once, centrally, rather than being independently interpreted by each consuming system.
