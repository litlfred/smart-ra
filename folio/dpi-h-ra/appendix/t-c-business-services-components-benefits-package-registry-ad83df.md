| **Stakeholder** | **Relationship to the registry** | **Value derived** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ministry of Health** | Would be the primary steward in most countries. Authors and governs the benefit package definitions that represent national health policy. | Single authoritative view of all health entitlements across schemes; capability to identify coverage gaps and policy-design evidence. |
| **National health insurance agency / Social Health Protection (SHP) body** | Scheme operator. Authors and manages package definitions for schemes under its mandate; accesses other schemes for cross-scheme coordination. | Authoritative package definitions anchoring operational enrolment, eligibility, contracting, and claims processes; reduced duplication with other payers. |
| **Private insurers** | Participating institution in multi-payer systems. May author and manage definitions for their schemes; queries public package definitions for coordination and anti-duplication. | Consistent national definitions supporting claims exchange and interoperability with government schemes. |
| **Healthcare providers** | Consuming institution. Queries published package definitions (via operational point-of-care or eligibility systems alongside the patient's active enrolment in the Beneficiary Registry) to see what services and products a scheme covers and under what general conditions. | Reliable, system-accessible coverage reference information that reduces manual verification, clarifies prior-authorisation requirements, and reduces denied-claims rates. |
| **Beneficiaries / patients** | End users of benefit entitlements. Access benefit definitions through beneficiary-facing portals, health wallets, and assisted-access channels that pair the registry's machine-readable definitions with plain-language, multilingual, accessible explanations. | Transparent, understandable, and accessible information about what they are entitled to, enabling informed engagement with services and accountability for what is delivered. |
| **Social protection / welfare agencies** | External eligibility data steward. Governs socioeconomic, income-assessment, or social-assistance classifications referenced declaratively by benefit definitions and queried by operational enrolment systems under data-minimisation safeguards. | Reduced data duplication; clear institutional boundary and privacy safeguards between health benefit definition, social-protection assessment, and scheme enrolment. |
| **Development partners and donors** | Scheme funders. Register and manage definitions for donor-financed benefit programmes alongside government schemes. | Visibility of their programmes within the national health benefit landscape; ability to coordinate coverage with government schemes. |
