- **Benefit package catalogue management.** Creation, maintenance, and publication of authoritative benefit package definitions, covering all scheme types within a country's financing ecosystem — insurance-based, government-funded, community-based, and donor-financed — without assuming any single financing model.
- **Benefit structure and coverage definition.** Structured publication of what each package covers: included services, procedures, diagnostics, medicines, and devices; exclusions; co-payment and cost-sharing parameters; utilisation caps; annual limits; and testable machine-readable reference rules where appropriate — while leaving negotiated provider tariffs, fee schedules, and executable claims adjudication logic to operational financing systems.
- **General eligibility criteria definition.** Definition and governance of the population-level eligibility criteria associated with each benefit package — including demographic, socioeconomic, employment-status, disease-specific, and geographic criteria — and support for declarative references to eligibility rules evaluated against external systems (social protection registries, poverty or income-assessment databases) without replicating external data or executing individual eligibility determinations inside the registry.
- **Multi-scheme and multi-payer association.** Management of the payer or funding source associated with each benefit package, supporting environments with multiple concurrent schemes operated by different institutions — public insurers, social health protection agencies, private insurers, and government programmes — within a single reference registry.
- **Facility tier and provider category linkage.** Support for associating benefit packages with the general facility types, care levels, service categories, and practitioner qualification types through which covered services may be accessed (referencing the Health Facility Registry and Health Workforce Registry), while keeping provider-specific empanelment, contracting, and service authorisation in operational Contract and Benefits Management Systems.
- **Coverage rules and limits publication.** Publication of service-level coverage parameters and testable rule artefacts — including coverage levels by facility tier, geographic availability, referral prerequisites, prior-authorisation indicators, and differential coverage conditions — for consumption by operational eligibility, pre-authorisation, and claims systems or a separate governed rules service.
- **Package lifecycle and version management.** Maintenance of full version history for benefit package definitions, with support for concurrent versions, defined lifecycle states, and backward-compatible referencing so that operational claims systems can validate retrospective claims against the package version in effect at the time of care.
- **Cross-scheme visibility and gap analysis.** Query capability enabling authorised users — Ministry of Health officials, health financing analysts, regulators — to view benefit coverage definitions across all registered schemes, identify population categories not covered by any package, and detect coverage overlaps across schemes.
