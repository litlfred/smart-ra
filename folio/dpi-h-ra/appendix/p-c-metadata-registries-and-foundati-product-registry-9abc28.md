- **Product identity, master data, and regulatory status management.** Creation, maintenance, and publication of governed product records covering the standardised core attributes that uniquely identify each product at generic (where applicable) and trade-item (brand/pack) level, together with mandatory regulatory status where regulation applies — explicitly supporting registered/authorised, exempt, unregistered/unregulated, emergency-use, donated, investigational, and pending categories.
- **Identifier validation, assignment, and level-aware cross-referencing.** Recording and validation of externally assigned global identifiers — such as Global Trade Item Numbers (GTINs) issued by authorised organisations under GS1 rules and ISO Unique Device Identifiers (UDIs) — rather than issuing global trade identifiers directly; assignment of national product codes only under appropriate national authority and governance; and maintenance of level-aware mappings between national and global identifiers that distinguish generic concepts, trade items, and packaging hierarchy levels, capturing mapping type, purpose, equivalence scope, validity, supporting evidence, provenance, and human review of ambiguous mappings.
- **Multi-scheme product classification.** Classification of products under applicable international and national coding schemes (e.g., UNSPSC for pharmaceuticals, GPC for medical devices, ATC and the WHODrug data dictionary for medicines, and ISO IDMP identifiers where adopted), supporting simultaneous classification under multiple schemes where required.
- **Packaging hierarchy and unit of measure management.** Definition and maintenance of the packaging levels for each product (unit, inner pack, case, pallet) with the associated externally assigned GTINs or national packaging codes and quantity conversions across levels, enabling consistent unit-of-measure handling across ordering, warehousing, and dispensing.
- **Conditional generic-to-trade-item mapping.** Where a meaningful generic concept exists for the product class (such as multi-source medicines, whereas certain medical devices, diagnostics, and unique commodities have no distinct generic-to-item relationship), management of the identity-grouping relationship between a generic product definition (e.g., amoxicillin 500mg capsule) and the specific branded trade items associated with it. This relationship serves as an input to clinical substitution and procurement equivalence assessments — which additionally require regulatory authorisation, quality assurance, formulation, strength, dosage form, route, packaging, and policy checks — rather than proof of clinical interchangeability or procurement suitability on its own.
- **Product lifecycle and change management.** Maintenance of full version history for product records, including capture of all changes with timestamps, source provenance, and submitter/approver attribution, and support for defined lifecycle states (draft, active, deprecated, suspended/withdrawn, retired).
- **Organisational trust, submission provenance, and attribute-specific governance workflows.** Role- and attribute-based access management, organisational and credential verification for external submitters, submission provenance capture, digital-signature support where appropriate, and strict separation of submitter and approver roles, paired with configurable attribute- and product-class-specific approval workflows across regulatory, clinical, logistics, and master-data stewardship authorities.
- **Mandatory bulk data onboarding and strategy-driven external source integration.** Mandatory bulk onboarding or equivalent migration capability — with validation, record matching, staging/dry-run preview, approval, and rollback — together with conditional system-to-system or scheduled integration with illustrative external authoritative sources (such as national regulatory authorities, WHO prequalification listings, national procurement and donor catalogues, medical-device registries, international product databases, manufacturers, the Global Data Synchronisation Network (GDSN), WHODrug, and ISO IDMP implementations), governed by a country source strategy that defines source-authority assessment, attribute-level precedence, verification, provenance, licensing compliance, reconciliation, and fallback mechanisms for every onboarded source.
