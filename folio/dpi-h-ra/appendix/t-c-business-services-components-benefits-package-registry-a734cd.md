| **GOVERNANCE** | Governance of the Benefits Package Registry follows the cross-cutting governance framework set out in Section 3.4 of the guidance document. Component-specific considerations include:<br>(1) The Benefits Package Registry has a uniquely complex multi-institutional governance context. Unlike registries with a single steward (such as the Health Facility Registry owned by the MoH), benefit package definitions may be authored by multiple institutions — the Ministry of Health, national health insurance agencies, private scheme operators, and donor-financed programme managers — each with legitimate authority over their own schemes. The governance framework should clearly define which institution has stewardship authority for each package type, and what approval is required before a package definition or reference rule artefact can be published or modified.<br>(2) Because eligibility for some benefit packages depends on data held in external social protection or income-assessment systems, the governance framework should address cross-institutional data referencing — including statutory mandates, data-sharing agreements, data-minimisation safeguards limiting personal data exchange to minimal eligibility assertions, refresh schedules when household circumstances change, and dispute resolution for cases where an external source returns inconsistent data.<br>(3) The registry should be designed to support the central oversight principle: central visibility without central control. Ministries of Health and regulators require aggregate visibility across all schemes to support coverage analysis and policy; this does not imply that they control the operational administration of individual schemes.<br>(4) The governance framework should maintain a strict boundary between publishing authoritative benefit definitions in the registry and executing consequential financing decisions in operational systems — specifying that country-specific CBMS implementations must reconcile enrolment and claims records against the shared Client Registry identity, defining where responsibility resides for assessing the sufficiency and provenance of clinical and service-delivery evidence, and pairing registry linkage with operational audit, anomaly detection, investigation, sanctions, appeal mechanisms, internal financial controls, and independent oversight. |
