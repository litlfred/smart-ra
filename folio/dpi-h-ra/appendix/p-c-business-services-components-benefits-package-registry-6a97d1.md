The registry is a metadata component in the precise architectural sense: it publishes authoritative coverage definitions and reference rules, not individual transactions or operational determinations. It does not enrol beneficiaries, determine person-specific eligibility, manage payer-provider contracts or empanelment, execute prior authorisation, adjudicate claims, or initiate payments. Those functions belong to operational systems (such as Beneficiary Registries, Contract and Benefits Management Systems, or separate governed rules services) that consume the registry as their reference source. What the registry provides is the stable, governed, agreed-upon specification of general entitlements — the canonical reference answer to what is covered, for which eligible population categories, under which scheme or payer, under what general conditions, and to what coverage limits — including which facility tiers, service categories, and practitioner qualification types are eligible to deliver covered services (referencing the Health Facility Registry and Health Workforce Registry rather than storing provider contracts), and which specific products are covered and under what conditions (referencing the Product Registry, including rules on generic medicine substitution or emergency alternatives). Operational systems then combine these published definitions with current person-specific enrolment records, provider contract and empanelment status, and contextual clinical or administrative data to make runtime decisions.
