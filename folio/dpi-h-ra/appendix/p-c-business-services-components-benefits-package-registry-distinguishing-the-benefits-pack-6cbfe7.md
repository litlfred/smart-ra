Note: The three components above are complementary, not competing. A functioning health financing system requires all three: the Benefits Package Registry publishes the authoritative *what* (covered services, general entitlement criteria, and machine-readable reference rules); the Contract and Benefits Management System executes the operational *how* (provider empanelment and contracting, tariff management, prior authorisation, claims adjudication, evidentiary review, and payment authorisation); and the Beneficiary Registry records the person-specific *who* (scheme enrolment and membership status). Conflating them — most commonly by building enrolment, provider contracting, or executable claims adjudication into the Benefits Package Registry, or by burying package definitions inside a scheme-specific claims system — produces fragmentation, duplication, and an inability to see the national coverage picture as a whole. Because CBMS and Beneficiary Registry implementations are country- and scheme-specific, any country-specific CBMS implementation should reconcile enrolment and claims records against the shared Client Registry identity and support a standard cross-scheme check for duplicate enrolment. This reconciliation provides a common interoperability baseline for eligibility verification and financial-integrity controls across schemes — supporting, rather than replacing, the operational audit, anomaly-detection, investigation, sanction, appeal, internal-control, and independent-oversight mechanisms required to address fraud, false claims, collusion, and leakage.
