Consent is the primary mechanism through which individuals — directly, with support, or through an authorised representative where permitted by law — exercise these rights: the ability to give, withhold, and withdraw permission for health information to be accessed and shared. As health information becomes increasingly digital and longitudinal — held in personal health records and exchanged across facilities and programmes — a shared means of recording and acting on an individual's consent preferences and governed delegations grows more important. Without it, the broader aims of individual access to health information and meaningful control over its use are difficult to realise in practice, and applications risk recreating proxy access inconsistently.
