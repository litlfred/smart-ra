These shared reference components do not, by themselves, run a financing system. Whereas the Benefits Package Registry publishes authoritative benefit definitions and machine-readable reference rules where appropriate, the operational execution of health financing is carried out by functional applications outside DPI-H — beneficiary registries and enrolment platforms, Contract and Benefits Management Systems (CBMS), claims adjudication engines, provider contract and tariff management tools, payroll systems, and public financial management (PFM) systems. Those operational systems draw on the shared registries to enrol beneficiaries, determine person-specific eligibility (including through governed cross-sector queries to social-protection and income-assessment systems under strict data-minimisation safeguards), manage provider empanelment and negotiated tariffs, execute prior authorisation and claims adjudication against clinical and service-delivery evidence, communicate entitlements to patients in plain, accessible language, authorise lawful payments before foundational payment rails move funds, and support financial integrity through audit, anomaly detection, investigation, appeal, and independent oversight. The architecture enables these operational systems rather than replacing them, and the shared reference components reduce the duplication and fragmentation they would otherwise carry. This pattern holds across all seven health goals.
