Countries should ensure that their architectural choices give concrete expression to the rights of individuals over their personal health data, and to the rights of public entities over the infrastructure and data they depend on. Consent management, access controls, and data governance should be enforceable, transparent, and meaningful in practice. Architectural decisions around storage, exchange, and access should default to data minimisation, purpose limitation, and individual agency — recognising that individuals may exercise that agency directly, with support under their direction, or through an authorised representative (such as a proxy, legal guardian, or designated caregiver), including during childhood, temporary illness, disability, cognitive decline, or other changes in functional ability — with any departure justified within the applicable legal framework.
