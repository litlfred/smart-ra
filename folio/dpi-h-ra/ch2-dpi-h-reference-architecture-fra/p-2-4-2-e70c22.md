The notable finding is how strongly health financing builds on shared infrastructure that other goals already require — the Client Registry to resolve canonical person identity for beneficiary and enrolment systems, the Health Facility Registry and Health Workforce Registry to verify facility licensing and practitioner credentials as inputs to provider empanelment and contracting, and the Product Registry and Terminology Services to supply standardised product and clinical codes referenced by pricing, tariff, and reimbursement processes. Within the component set and scope adopted by this reference architecture, the principal additional shared reference component emerging from the financing analysis is the Benefits Package Registry: an authoritative publication of covered services, scheme benefit definitions, general entitlement criteria, and machine-readable reference rules where appropriate, which operational eligibility, contracting, and purchasing functions across the system rely on.
