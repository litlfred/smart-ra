Public health infrastructure is increasingly built and operated through a mix of public, private, donor, and partner arrangements, and a government can hold formal ownership of a system while lacking the practical ability to access its data, modify it, or move away from the supplier that runs it. The architecture should be designed so that this practical control sits with the public entity by default — through open standards, documented interfaces, data portability, and clear separation between the data a country owns and the systems that process it — so that a country can exercise genuine control over its essential infrastructure and data, rather than that control being constrained by the arrangements through which those systems are funded and operated.
