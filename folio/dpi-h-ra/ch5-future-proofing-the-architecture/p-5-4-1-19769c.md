Country-level legislation and organisational policies governing AI — covering data protection, the configuration of input and output safeguards, the quality and representativeness of training data, and the monitoring of model drift over time — are practical prerequisites for moving from pilots to routine use. WHO's guidance on the ethics and governance of AI for health<sup>44</sup>, and its guidance on large multi-modal models<sup>45</sup>, set out principles and recommendations that countries can draw on, and several jurisdictions are establishing legal frameworks of their own.43, \cite{coe-cets-225-ai-convention,korea-2025-ai-basic-act,peru-2023-ley-31814}
