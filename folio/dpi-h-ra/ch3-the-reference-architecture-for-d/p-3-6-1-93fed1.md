A related distinction matters for clarity. Connecting two digital applications directly through an agreed message format achieves integration, but not necessarily interoperability. Interoperability, in its true sense, rests on open, publicly governed specifications that any conforming application component can implement, enabling one-to-many reuse and economies of scale rather than a proliferation of bilateral connections that accumulate technical debt and do not scale nationally. The objective of the architecture is interoperability in this stronger sense: shared specifications, openly governed, to which systems conform at their boundaries.
