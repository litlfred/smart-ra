The components are organised across two layers of the reference architecture, shown together in this integrated view (Figure 3.5). For readers who are not enterprise architects, the operational workflow connecting the **foundational shared data layer** (the metadata registries, Lifelong Health Record, Terminology Service, and LIMR) to the **business services layer** and to point-of-care applications follows a straightforward five-step pattern during a routine clinical or public-health interaction:

```
[1. Point-of-Service Application (e.g. Clinic EMR, Pharmacy, Lab, CHW App)]
       │
       ├──► [2. Foundational Registries (Client, Workforce, Facility, Product)]
       │         Resolves who the patient is, which health worker and facility
       │         deliver care, and which medicines or commodities are used
       │
       ├──► [3. Semantic & Longitudinal Core (Terminology Service, LIMR, LHR)]
       │         Validates standard clinical codes and data structures, and
       │         reads/updates the patient's longitudinal health record
       │
       └──► [4. Business Services Layer (CDSE, BPR, PHSP, HMIS, Supplier Registry)]
                 Applies computable clinical decision support, checks benefit
                 entitlements, triggers surveillance alerts, and aggregates indicators
```

1. **Identity and context resolution (Foundational Registries → Point of Service):** When a person seeks care or a service is initiated, the point-of-service application queries the **Client Registry**, **Health Workforce Registry**, **Health Facility Registry**, and (where medicines, vaccines, or devices are involved) the **Product Registry** to resolve authoritative identifiers for the patient, practitioner, care setting, and health product.
2. **Semantic validation and longitudinal record access (Semantic Core & LHR ↔ Point of Service):** Entries are structured according to models governed in the **Logical Information Model Repository (LIMR)** and coded against value sets served by the **Terminology Service**, allowing the encounter to be read from and recorded into the **Lifelong Health Record (LHR)** with full provenance and patient consent.
3. **Real-time clinical and entitlement support (Foundational Layer → Business Services → Point of Service):** Business-service components consume that resolved, coded context: the **Computable Decision Support Engine (CDSE)** evaluates the patient's coded record against computable clinical guidelines to return point-of-care recommendations; the **Benefits Package Registry (BPR)** provides the covered services and entitlement rules that financing and clinical systems apply; and the **Supplier Registry** links authorised suppliers to the products recorded in the Product Registry.
4. **Population-level surveillance and management (Foundational Layer → Public Health & Analytics Business Services):** From the same standardised events and registry identifiers, the **Public Health Surveillance Platform (PHSP)** detects notifiable conditions and outbreak signals, while the **Health Management Information System (HMIS)** compiles aggregate indicators for programme monitoring, workforce planning, and resource allocation — without requiring health workers to re-enter data into separate reporting forms.
