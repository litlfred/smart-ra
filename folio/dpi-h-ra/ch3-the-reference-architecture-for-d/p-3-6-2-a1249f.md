Moving data reliably is necessary but not sufficient; the data also needs to carry its meaning intact. Two complementary concerns determine whether it does: the *structure* of the data — how clinical facts are organised, qualified and related to one another — and the *meaning* of the coded values that the structure contains. A single code for "blood pressure", for example, conveys little without the structure that situates it: systolic and diastolic components, the body site, and the patient's state at the time of measurement. Structure without governed meaning, and meaning without governed structure, each leave data that a receiving system is unable to interpret reliably.
