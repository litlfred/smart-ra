Where the European infrastructure illustrates federated exchange of clinical content within a region, a complementary global initiative addresses the trust on which any cross-border exchange depends. WHO’s Global Digital Health Certification Network (GDHCN) is an openly governed trust network through which participating authorities verify the authenticity and provenance of verifiable health documents issued by one another — confirming, through a shared public-key infrastructure, that a document was genuinely issued by a trusted authority and has not been altered.[^36] It establishes trust without centralising data: the personal content of a document remains with the issuing authority, and the network conveys only the means to verify it. And it applies the same trust mechanism across many document types, keeping the trust layer separate from — and reusable across — the clinical content it secures, in the same way that, within a country, trust and transport are kept separate from the data they carry.
