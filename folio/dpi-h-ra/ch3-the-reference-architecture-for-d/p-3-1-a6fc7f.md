An architecture describes a system in terms of its elements, the relationships among them, and the principles guiding its design and evolution. For architectures to be compared or assessed against one another, they need a shared way of describing these things: agreed types of elements, agreed types of relationship between them, and consistent rules for how they fit together. Where such a shared description is absent, familiar terms such as “component”, “service”, and “capability” can carry different meanings in different settings, which makes systematic comparison difficult and limits the value of cross-country analysis.
