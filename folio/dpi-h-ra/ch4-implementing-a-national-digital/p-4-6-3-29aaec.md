Whatever the mix, financing plans should explicitly budget for the **inclusion and accessibility workstream** across the lifecycle of the architecture — including offline-first and low-bandwidth connectivity for rural and mountainous facilities, accessibility compliance and representative testing, multilingual and cross-language support, structured digital-competency training for health workers, patient navigation, and the sustained operation of assisted and non-digital routes of equivalent standing — so that cost savings are never achieved by shifting administrative burdens or exclusion onto underserved populations or frontline staff. The value case also has to be evidenced: the benefits case set out in the roadmap (section 4.1) and the progress and distributional equity measures in section 4.4 — coverage, standards conformance, governance maturity, and outcome linkage — provide the basis for showing Ministries of Finance and partners what shared infrastructure is delivering in return for its recurrent cost, and for sustaining the case for continued investment.
