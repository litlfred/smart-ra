Profiles and specifications are useful only if compliance with them can be verified. Countries should establish minimum conformance requirements for each DPI-H component and define the testing mechanisms through which conformance is assessed. An interoperability test bed[^41] — an openly available, standards-based testing environment offering validators, simulators, and test-management tooling — supports FHIR conformance testing and can be adapted to country-specific implementation profiles, giving countries a shared means of verification rather than one rebuilt from scratch for each procurement. As part of the inclusion and accessibility workstream, procurement requirements, conformance testing, and pre-rollout readiness assessments should also verify accessibility across functional abilities (aligned with standards such as WCAG and ITU-T F.780.2), multilingual and cross-language data capture, offline-first and low-bandwidth operation, representative usability testing with health workers of varying digital literacy and with underserved patient groups, and the operational readiness of assisted and non-digital routes of equivalent standing for every function on which care access, rights, or health entitlements depend. Countries should consider requiring both technical and inclusion conformance certification against published profiles as a condition of procurement approval and deployment go/no-go decisions for DPI-H components, so that every system entering the national digital health ecosystem advances both interoperability and equitable access.
