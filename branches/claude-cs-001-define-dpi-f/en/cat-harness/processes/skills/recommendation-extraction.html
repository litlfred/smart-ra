recommendation-extraction

Skill recommendation-extraction: Extract the recommendations of an L1 source as L1 3.0 recommendation and remark nodes, then describe each one’s scenario: the persona (role) who acts on it, the business process they act in, and the user story, as smart-kg L2 user-scenario candidates that reuse the target guide’s own vocabulary. Read before extracting recommendations from a WHO guideline or position paper with no printed “Recommendation N” labels, before linking a recommendation to a DAK persona or process, and whenever a recommendation has to say who does it and where. Covers the hybrid extraction (mechanical candidates, agent structuring, verbatim and 100 % coverage gates), the reuse-first precedence for personas and processes, the WARNING format, and sign-off.

Named by: Classify and code content, SMART Guideline end to end

Skill id: recommendation-extraction · Package: authoring-who-smart-guidelines · Follows dak-l1-library, which builds the library and the layout graph this extracts from.

A recommendation that does not say who does it, and during what, cannot be implemented. This skill extracts each recommendation verbatim and records, per recommendation, the persona who acts on it, the business process they act in, and whether the guideline is clinical, public-health or health-system-strengthening, before any L2 asset is drawn. It reuses existing vocabulary and processes first, and warns when none fits.

Input and output

The contract is input.schema.json and output.schema.json.

  • In: l1Sources, dak-l1-library’s output, and the targetGuide whose personas and processes the scenarios reuse first.
  • Out: l1Kg: per included source, its extractionPath (library/<bib>/l1-extraction/), sourceVersion, the recommendation, remark, practice and scenario-candidate counts, the coverage and every WARNING. Stages 3 (l2-dependency-choice), 4 and 5 take it as their l1Kg.

As stage 2 of smart-guideline-end-to-end, a source is skipped only when it is already in a KG: its extraction directory exists, is declared, and is at the same source version (skipped: true). A library entry with no extraction still needs it, after bun run cat ingest and dak-l1-library’s generic-document layer have put it in library/. Skipping is per source.

The two halves

half produces layer schema
extraction recommendation and remark nodes, verbatim — or, for implementation guidance, implementation-practice nodes L1 L1 3.0, smart-base/kg/src/l1.ts (KGRecommendation); implementation-practice is the l1-library extension, smart-base/kg/src/l1-library.ts (KGImplementationPractice); not smart-kg 1.0
scenario one user-scenario candidate per recommendation, joined to persona and business-process L2 smart-kg ontology/l2/l2.json, unchanged; smart-base UserScenario.fsh

No L1 field carries the scenario: L1 holds what WHO says; who does it, and during which process, is adaptation, which is L2’s.

Implementation guidance prints practices, not recommendations

A recommendation is a GRC-approved normative statement made by a guideline. A publication whose publicationType is implementation-guidance or methodology (a “how to” document, such as a digital transformation handbook with its own “Best practices” lists) makes no recommendations of its own (handbook for guideline development §1.9). What it prints as normative is an implementation-practice node, never a recommendation with a new kind code.

  • Record the type in the curation as publication.publicationType. With implementation-guidance or methodology, every as: recommendation decision emits an implementation-practice node at <publication>/implementation-practice/<key>, and the graph names l1-library.context.jsonld so the validator scopes L1 + the extension.
  • The class lives in smart-base/kg/src/l1-library.ts; l1.ts stays the L1 3.0 layer, unextended.
  • It carries identifier, statement (verbatim) and contentHash; no kind, direction, strength or certainty. A kind on such a decision is refused.
  • No remark. L1 3.0’s remark is minted under a recommendation, so a remark decision in such a publication is refused: exclude the sentence or make it its own practice.
  • smart-base/kg/src/validate.ts refuses a recommendation defined in an implementation-guidance or methodology publication.
  • The output graph is l1.json.

Part 1 — extraction (hybrid)

Candidates are mechanical, structure is the agent’s, and the gates are mechanical.

  1. Ingest the source (see dak-l1-library steps 1–2): bun run cat ingest uploads/<doc_id>/<doc_id>.pdf --library <lib>.
  2. Fast path — a guideline that prints labels. When the source prints Recommendation N: labels, bun run smart-base/scripts/extract-smart-kg-l1.ts --entry smart-base/library/<id> extracts them by label (it writes L1 1.0). A source with no labels, such as the measles position paper, takes the hybrid path below.

  3. List the normative sentences. Run core’s l1-coverage with an empty capture list; every sentence carrying a marker (should, shall, must, recommend*, should not, may be given/administered/offered/…) comes back as unaccounted, with its page:

    echo '[]' > captured.json
bun run cat l1:coverage --text pages.json --captured captured.json --out candidates.json

    --text is body text only, page-tagged; strip references and running headers first.

  4. Structure them. The agent turns each candidate into an L1 3.0 recommendation (identifier, statement, kind, population, intervention, setting, …) or a remark (text, remarkType) attached by hasRemark:
    • statement and text are verbatim: the printed words, line breaks joined, nothing else changed;
    • a sentence that is history, background or a manufacturer’s statement is proposed for exclusion (step 5), not forced into a node;
    • GRADE strength and certainty are set only where the source prints them;
    • every node is inferred, with a note and evidence (page and quote).

  5. Gate: 100 % accounted for. Re-run with the captured statements and the exclusions:

    bun run cat l1:coverage --text pages.json --captured captured.json \
  --exclusions exclusions.json --out coverage.json

    Exit 0 only at 100 %. An exclusion counts only with signedOffBy: an agent may propose one, only a person signs it, and the reason is one of background-fact, manufacturer-statement, editorial, research-question or duplicate-of:<id>.

  6. Gate: the graph’s shape. Validate the L1 3.0 document with the Zod port: npx tsx src/validate.ts <graph.json> from smart-base/kg/.

Part 1 as a tool: l1-recommendation-candidates

Steps 3–6 are the named skill recommendation-candidates, satisfied by the Tool smart-base/tools/l1-recommendation-candidates/, run twice around the agent’s judgement:

# 1. mechanical: the work list — every normative sentence, with hints; nothing decided
bun run smart-base/scripts/l1-recommendation-candidates.ts --entry smart-base/library/<id> --out <dir>
# 2. agent: write <dir>/curation.yaml (l1-recommendation-curation/v1), one decision per location
# 3. mechanical: the gates, and the L1 3.0 graph
bun run smart-base/scripts/l1-recommendation-candidates.ts --entry smart-base/library/<id> \
  --curation <dir>/curation.yaml --out <dir> [--check]

The curation is the agent’s step. The script hints (licence-or-imprint, glossary-page, layout-fragment) and proposes the publication’s identity from the printed ISBN; it never decides. Per candidate location (p<page>:s<n>, l1-coverage’s own), the agent records one of:

as means fields
recommendation an L1 recommendation node key; statement only to narrow a joined list to one bullet, or to join a split sentence; kind only where printed
remark a remark of a recommendation, by hasRemark of, remarkType
part-of inside another recommendation’s statement of
exclude a PROPOSED exclusion, one of the fixed reasons reason; signedOffBy is a person’s acting as l1-extraction-reviewer, never an agent’s

Every decision carries a basis. A location may carry a list of recommendation decisions: the splitter reads a bulleted list as one sentence.

It refuses a statement not printed in the entry (normalised), an override that neither contains nor is contained in its candidate, a location the entry does not hold, a reused key, and a remark of an undeclared key, then checks the graph with smart-base/kg/src/validate.ts. It writes recommendation-candidates.json (the work list), l1.json, exclusions.proposed.json, extraction-warnings.md and run-context.json.

Who signs an exclusion. Each record in exclusions.proposed.json names its signer as a role-graph RoleRef (cat-harness-tools/schemas/process/role-graph.ts): "signerRole": {"role": "l1-extraction-reviewer"}, declared in smart-base/scenarios/roles.json (a person, inheriting the harness’s reviewer). It stays unsigned until a person in that role reads the sentence against the printed page and signs it in Lane_ExtractionReviewer of classify-and-code-content.bpmn, following classify-and-code-content and terminology-coding.

Provenance. Each run of either tool writes a cat-harness folio-run-context/v1 (cat-harness-tools/schemas/sdlc/run-context.ts) as run-context.json beside its output: the platform commit, the mounts, the script and its hash, and the inputs’ hash basis, as pdf-ocr.py writes at ingest. The work list cites it by reference. --check ignores it, as it ignores generatedAt.

Declare every output directory, in the same commit. Each --out directory in or beside a library (library/<entry>/l1-extraction/, a sibling such as library/l1-recommendation-scenarios/ that Part 2 writes, or l1-extractions/<entry>/) is declared in the instance’s <name>.json with "graphTypologies": ["l1-extraction"], one entry per directory, never a wildcard (smart-base’s l1-extraction-9789240116191-eng is the pattern). An undeclared one fails folio-assistant’s check:l1-complete, and with it the QA working copy, every test shard and gate reading it, and the staging build. When the entry lives in another instance’s library, hold the output at l1-extractions/<entry>/, never in a library/<entry>/ folder: that reads as a duplicate of the entry, which the library ingest refuses. Before staging, run bun run cat check:l1-complete in a folio-assistant checkout that mounts the instance at the commit being staged; it must exit 0.

Part 2 — the scenario

Its mechanical step is the named skill recommendation-scenarios (Tool l1-recommendation-scenarios).

The shape: L2 nodes, reused unchanged

Per recommendation, one user-scenario (smart-base UserScenario: title, id, description, personas[]) joined by three edges smart-kg L2 already licenses:

recommendation   implementedBy  user-scenario
user-scenario    involves       persona
business-process realises       user-scenario

The description is a user story:

As a <persona>, during <process>, I <act> so that <outcome>.

Each link records its source, as an intake classifications entry does (cat-harness-tools/schemas/library/intake.ts):

source meaning smart-kg derivation
declared a person said so; names by decided
context the section the statement is printed under settles it inferred
inferred an agent’s reading of the statement inferred

and a status: candidate until a person reviews the entry.

Reuse first, stop at the first hit

Persona, business-process and guideline-domain coding apply the terminology-coding methodology (backed by classify-and-code-content, the terminology-lookup Tool, and glossary-terms):

what 1st 2nd otherwise
persona the target guide’s own personas smart-base’s generic DAK.Persona.* (input/fsh/actors/, ISCO / CDHI) a glossary candidate, and a WARNING
process the target guide’s own processes a domain process catalogue — none exists yet (smart-base-clinical holds no processes): report “catalogue absent” could-not-determine, and a WARNING
domain clinical | public-health | health-system-strengthening, per scenario, with its source    

Never invent a process. A process not in the guide is could-not-determine, and the warning says why: it is the evidence that the guide (or a catalogue) is missing one. Choose the persona who acts on the statement, not the one it is about: “Vaccine should be offered to travellers” is acted on by the health worker. A persona a guide’s description does not cover (an immunization DAK’s health worker treating measles) falls to the next rung rather than being stretched.

Read the statement, not its section: “Countries should achieve ≥95 % coverage” sits under WHO position and is acted on through coverage analysis (IMMZ.I), while “MCVs should not be given to individuals with … severe immunosuppression” is a per-client check (IMMZ.D, IMMZ.D5). A national policy, schedule-design or programme decision is not a per-client process.

Run it

  1. The vocabulary. For the target guide, read its business-processes.md and personas.md from a guide checkout; for the generic rung, read smart-base’s ActorDefinitions:

    bun run smart-base/scripts/l1-scenario-vocabulary.ts --guide <guide checkout> --out guide-vocabulary.json
bun run smart-base/scripts/l1-scenario-vocabulary.ts --fsh-actors <smart-base checkout>/input/fsh/actors --out smart-base-generic-personas.json

    Each file records the repository, the commit, and the file and line of every entry. The guide’s name, namespace and repository come from the checkout’s <name>.json and sushi-config.yaml canonical. Persona ids are slug(title) where the guide prints none (as smart-kg kgid.mjs mints a persona IRI); process ids are the printed Process IDs.

  2. The mapping (l1-scenario-mapping/v1, YAML), authored: per recommendation, its personas (an id, or a candidate term), its process (an id, or could-not-determine), act, outcome and domain, each with a source and a basis quoting the statement, the vocabulary row or the person’s ruling.

  3. The candidates:

    bun run smart-base/scripts/l1-recommendation-scenarios.ts \
  --l1 <recommendations.yaml | l1-graph.json> --vocabulary guide-vocabulary.json \
  --generic smart-base-generic-personas.json --mapping mapping.yaml --out <dir> [--check]

    It writes scenario-candidates.l2.json (an L2 graph document) and scenario-warnings.md, and refuses:

    • an id the vocabulary does not hold;
    • a recommendation with no mapping entry (coverage is 100 %);
    • a mapping entry for a recommendation the source does not hold;
    • a declared link that names nobody.
  4. Tier 2. node tools/validate.mjs <l1-graph.json> scenario-candidates.l2.json in a smart-kg checkout; give it the L1 document too, or every implementedBy edge reads as dangling.

The WARNING format

One line per warning, greppable, in the report and on stderr:

WARNING process-not-determined <key>: no <guide> process (<ids>) realises this; domain process catalogue absent. <basis>
WARNING persona-glossary-candidate <key>: persona "<term>" is a glossary candidate — … <basis>

A warning is not a refusal: the run succeeds, and the warnings are the work list for whoever owns the guide’s processes or the glossary.

Review and sign-off

Everything this skill writes is a candidate. A person reviews an entry and records it in the mapping (review: {by, at}); the run then marks that entry’s links reviewed. A person may also turn a link declared (with by). An agent never writes either. Part 1’s exclusions are signed only by a person acting as l1-extraction-reviewer.

What smart-kg L2 could not hold

user-scenario declares only id, title, description, sourceKind, source and resolutionStatus, and tier 2 refuses any other node property. So domain, each link’s linkSource and the reviewStatus go on the edges’ open properties; the act and outcome live only inside description.

What it feeds, later

The scenarios are the hinge to L2. Each realises edge names the process, and so the BPMN whose lane is the persona, a scenario will be drawn in; the recommendation’s conditions become DMN rules (for measles, the IMMZ.D2.DT.Measles.* and IMMZ.D5 tables); and the user story becomes a functional requirement (functional-requirement fulfilledBy persona). None of that is drawn here. could-not-determine scenarios have no process to draw into yet.

The worked examples

An ingested handbook, end to end. smart-base/l1-extractions/9789240116191-eng/ is the Digital transformation handbook for health product catalogue (WHO, 2025; its sections are in who-iris/library/9789240116191-eng/) run through both tools: 48 normative sentences. It is implementation guidance, so the curation took 22 implementation practices and proposed 30 exclusions (licence text, glossary definitions, background on SMART Guidelines, chapter checkpoints, and the definition of the data owner on page 26), each naming l1-extraction-reviewer as signer and none signed, so coverage reads 18/48 until a person signs them. The scenarios reuse the handbook’s own roles (data owner, custodian, steward, project manager, solution architect: 18 links), one generic DAK.Persona.DataManager, and 3 glossary candidates (the national regulatory authority, twice, and the master data governance group). The handbook prints no business process with an id and no health-system catalogue exists, so all 22 are could-not-determine: 26 warnings, one of them practice-implementedBy-unlicensed (smart-kg’s L2 licenses implementedBy from a recommendation only, so from a practice it is a proposal for L2). The test l1-recommendation-candidates.test.ts keeps it current.

An authored L1, scenario half only. The 75 recommendations of the WHO measles position paper (WER 92(17), 2017), mapped against the immunization guide’s personas and processes, with the vocabulary, mapping, output and warnings, live in that guide’s library with the check that keeps them current: https://github.com/litlfred/smart-immunizations/tree/main/library/l1-recommendation-scenarios/measles. smart-base holds no guide’s example. The generic rung it was run with is smart-base/scripts/fixtures/l1-recommendation-scenarios/smart-base-generic-personas.json, shared with the handbook example.

An authored L1 from pictures. When the source prints its recommendations only as images (a schedule poster, a slide deck), Part 1 finds nothing and the L1 is authored from the inspected image narratives and transcriptions of the rendered page. Its check is the step skill transcription-verbatim (Tool l1-transcription-verbatim): every statement verbatim in the narrative or transcription it names, every transcription word corroborated by the text layer unless listed under inspectionOnly with its basis. The Uganda schedule poster and UNEPI deck (https://github.com/litlfred/smart-immunizations-uganda/tree/main/library/l1-recommendation-scenarios/uganda) are the worked example.
